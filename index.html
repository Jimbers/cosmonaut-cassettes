What are the Cosmonaut Cassettes?

In 1970, engineer Anya Lapteva fled the Soviet Union. She predicted Nixon’s visit to China to the hour years ahead and claimed to know this because she had encountered prophetic cassette tapes the Soviet Space Program had recovered on one of their missions.


The only known photo of Anya Lapteva

Less than a year later, she died, and nearly all physical evidence of her existence was scrubbed from the planet in an unprecedented collective effort by Eastern Bloc and Western intelligence agencies. You can learn more about her from this website.

Little more came out about the tapes until after the Soviet Union collapsed, and even what we have learned since then is murky. They were held at the Baikonur Cosmodrome but lost in the chaos. Some claimed most were destroyed, though others say enough backups were made that all can still be found.


Sergei Krikalev, who was in space when the USSR collapsed. He was stranded in space for almost a year.

Ever since, odd mentions of the tapes and recordings of the songs would show up online before quickly disappearing, with posters who shared actual songs regularly disappearing. I have been following this for years, trying to archive what I could.

After a great deal of effort, I have found some of them. I also have a large part of the original research notes: translations, transcripts, and the commentary of the scientists and soldiers who found them.

I will not tell you how I found them, or how many of the tapes I have, but I will share what I can as it becomes safe to do so.

Please save copies.
